# Question 1: Sourcing Methods

One of the biggest lessons I learned while working on Part A was that finding manufacturing companies is not difficult. The difficult part is finding companies that genuinely match the Federer profile. Many companies manufacture products, but far fewer have strong technical leadership, differentiated products, evidence of growth and the ability to build long-term competitive advantages.

Because of this, I would not rely on Google searches alone. Instead, I would use a combination of databases, industry ecosystems, certifications, founder research and indirect sourcing methods. Each method reveals a different signal, and the strongest candidates are usually the companies that appear across multiple signals.

## 1. Certification-Based Sourcing

The first method I would use is certification databases such as AS9100, IATF 16949, NABL, DSIR and ISO 13485.

During Part A, I noticed that many of the strongest companies had invested heavily in quality systems and process discipline. Certifications often require significant effort, documentation, audits and long-term commitment. For example, aerospace companies frequently hold AS9100 certifications, while automotive suppliers often maintain IATF 16949 standards.

What makes this useful is that certifications act as a shortcut to operational maturity. Instead of reviewing thousands of companies from scratch, I can start with businesses that have already demonstrated a certain level of manufacturing capability and process excellence.

However, certifications alone do not guarantee a good Federer company. Some businesses may be certified but still operate in commodity segments or show limited growth. Therefore, certifications would be used as a starting point rather than a final qualification method.

## 2. Industry Association Directories

I would also use industry associations such as ACMA, SIDM, EEPC India, Instrumentation Society of India and various engineering and electronics associations.

Associations are valuable because they naturally bring together companies operating in specialized sectors. Many excellent manufacturers are not highly visible online but are active participants within their industry ecosystem. During my research, I found that some of the most interesting companies were discovered through industry networks rather than conventional searches.

Association membership also suggests that a company is engaged with industry developments, regulations and customer needs. It provides access to businesses that are serious enough about their sector to participate in professional communities.

The limitation is that membership quality can vary widely, so additional research is always necessary.

## 3. Trade Shows and Industrial Exhibitions

Trade shows would be another major sourcing channel. Examples include Aero India, Automation Expo, IMTEX, DEFEXPO, Electronica India and India Manufacturing Show.

I particularly like this method because exhibitors are often companies that are actively investing in growth. Businesses spend money to participate in exhibitions because they want customers, partnerships, visibility and new opportunities. This creates a naturally filtered pool of companies that are usually more ambitious than average.

For example, an aerospace tooling company exhibiting at Aero India is often a stronger candidate than a generic manufacturer discovered through a simple search. Exhibitor lists are also organized by industry, making it easier to focus on sectors that align with the Federer framework.

The downside is that some excellent companies never participate in trade shows, especially those that rely heavily on long-term customer relationships.

## 4. Export and Trade Intelligence Databases

I would use platforms such as Volza, ImportGenius, Panjiva and ExportGenius.

Export activity is one of the strongest indicators of manufacturing capability because exporting requires much more than simply producing a product. Companies must meet quality standards, maintain consistency, handle documentation and compete against international alternatives.

When I see a company exporting specialized products repeatedly, it immediately suggests a certain level of operational maturity. Export records can also provide insights into product categories, customer concentration and growth patterns.

Not every great company exports, but exporters often have stronger systems and more differentiated products than purely local manufacturers.

## 5. Supplier Ecosystem Mining

Another method I would use is supplier ecosystem research.

Instead of searching directly for manufacturers, I would identify companies supplying organizations such as ISRO, DRDO, HAL, Tata Motors, Mahindra, Bharat Forge and L&T.

The reason this works is simple. These organizations already spend significant time evaluating suppliers before approving them. If a company is trusted to supply critical components, systems or technologies to such organizations, that is already a strong signal of quality.

This approach allows me to leverage the work that sophisticated procurement teams have already done. Rather than evaluating every company from scratch, I can focus on businesses that have already demonstrated competence in demanding environments.

The challenge is that supplier information is not always publicly available and sometimes requires deeper investigation.

## 6. Founder-First LinkedIn Research

One of the most interesting observations from Part A was the importance of technical founders.

Several strong companies were built by former scientists, engineers or manufacturing experts who transformed their expertise into businesses. Because of this, I would use LinkedIn not only to search for companies but also to search for founders.

I would specifically look for individuals with backgrounds in aerospace, defence, manufacturing, automation, research institutions and engineering organizations. Examples could include former ISRO scientists, IIT graduates, PhD researchers or industry veterans.

Many differentiated businesses are built around the knowledge and experience of their founders. By identifying the right people first and then researching their companies, it becomes possible to discover businesses that traditional databases may overlook.

## 7. Industrial Cluster Mapping

I would also use industrial cluster mapping.

India's strongest manufacturing companies are often concentrated in specific regions such as Chakan, Pimpri-Chinchwad, Hosur, Coimbatore, Rajkot, Ahmedabad, Hyderabad and Bengaluru Aerospace Park.

One thing I noticed during research was that some excellent manufacturers had surprisingly basic websites. If I relied only on online visibility, I would have missed them completely.

Industrial clusters help solve this problem because they bring together suppliers, customers, skilled workers and supporting industries. By systematically mapping companies within these clusters, it becomes easier to uncover hidden manufacturing champions.

## 8. Patent and R&D Databases

Finally, I would use patent databases and R&D-related sources such as Google Patents, WIPO, the Indian Patent Office and DSIR-recognized R&D organizations.

One of the strongest Federer signals is differentiation. Companies with proprietary products, unique processes or significant R&D investments often have stronger competitive positions than commodity manufacturers.

Patent activity is not perfect because many great companies never file patents, but it provides another useful signal when evaluating innovation and technical depth.

## Conclusion

If I were sourcing Federer-profile companies across India, I would combine certification databases, industry associations, trade-show exhibitor lists, export databases, supplier ecosystem research, founder-focused LinkedIn analysis, industrial cluster mapping and innovation databases.

The biggest takeaway from Part A is that no single source consistently identifies the best companies. The strongest candidates usually appear across multiple signals. A company that holds relevant certifications, participates in industry ecosystems, exports specialized products, supplies major OEMs and is led by a technical founder is far more likely to match the Federer profile than a company discovered through a simple Google search. Using multiple sourcing methods together would allow me to find high-quality companies much more efficiently and with greater confidence.
